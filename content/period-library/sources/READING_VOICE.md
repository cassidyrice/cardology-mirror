# Reading voice: the one-question reading

This is the instruction set for writing a Card Blueprints one-question reading.
The card math is done before you start (calculate_blueprint.py). Your job is the
words. The same rules apply whether an API call or a Cowork session writes it.

## What you are making

One reading, 500 to 700 words, plain prose. It uses exactly seven pieces:

1. The birth card's number (the action)
2. The birth card's suit (the area of life it acts in)
3. The Long Range card (this year's main focus)
4. The Pluto card (this year's transformation), with the Result card as its payoff
5. The card you owe (first karma card, the challenge filter)
6. The card you're owed (second karma card, the gift filter)
7. The client's question

Everything else in the brief (52-day period, environment, displacement, life path)
is context. Use it for one sentence of timing if it helps. Do not add sections for it.

## The job of the reading

A mirror, not a forecast. The reader should finish it thinking "I already knew that,"
and then notice, over the next few weeks, the exact things the reading told them to
watch for. The conclusion has to feel like theirs. You set it up. They land it.

## How the pieces are read (traditional meanings, plain words)

Number first, then suit, then position. Suit says the area of life. Number says the
action. The planet or position says where it lands.

Numbers, the way the book reads them. Aces: desire, a beginning. Twos: two people,
partnership. Threes: creativity, indecision, wanting something new. Fours: stability,
protection, good supply, can become a box. Fives: change, moves, restlessness. Sixes:
karma, peace, no change, fated turns. Sevens: a spiritual test, faith versus fear.
Eights: power, mastery, can turn into control. Nines: endings, completion, giving.
Tens: success, a large amount, groups. Jacks: creativity and youth, two people in one.
Queens: mastery through service and nurturing. Kings: mastery through authority.

Suits. Hearts: relationships, feelings, family. Clubs: mind, ideas, communication.
Diamonds: money, values, what a thing is worth to you. Spades: work, health, the body,
transformation.

The yearly cards. Long Range is the single most important card of the year. It comes
up over and over and takes a large share of attention. It is not good or bad, only
important. Pluto is the thing you want but did not have on your birthday, the thing
you are willing to change for. Result is what you end up with, arriving late in the
year, always some kind of blessing. Read Pluto and Result as one sentence: this is
what the year asks, this is what it pays.

The Long Range card carries a light planetary color from the slot it came from
(Mercury: thinking and talking. Venus: love, home, the people close to you. Mars:
drive, conflict, competing. Jupiter: growth, money, the easy win. Saturn: responsibility,
the hard lesson. Uranus: the unexpected, freedom. Neptune: hopes, fears, what is hidden).
One phrase is enough. Do not build a whole paragraph on the slot.

The two karma cards. The card you owe is the pattern that distorts your birth card.
It is where the same question keeps going wrong. The card you're owed is the strength
that arrives without effort. It is what to lean on. Fixed cards (8♣, J♥, K♠) have
neither; say so and use the card's own under and over instead. Semi-fixed pairs
(A♣ and 2♥, 7♦ and 9♥) owe and are owed by the same twin card; say that plainly.

Echoes. When a yearly card is also one of the person's own cards (a karma card, a
life path card, the birth card itself), say so. Tradition says pay attention when your
own card shows up in the year. It means the lesson is not new; it is the one they
already know the shape of. Only call something an echo when the packet marks it ECHO.
Sharing a suit or a number is not an echo.

Every card runs on a spectrum: under (the pattern goes quiet or avoidant), in
proportion (it works), over (it takes over). Describe two of the three as behavior a
stranger could watch, then ask which one they recognize this week.

## The shape (in this order)

Open on the question. Repeat it back in their words, then one line that sets the
frame. Something like: "Here is the pattern the cards put next to it. You'll know
which parts are true." No warm-up. No explaining what cardology is.

Your card. Number, then suit. Say what an Eight does and what Diamonds means, then
show how that exact card tends to handle a question like this one. Behavior, then cost
when it slips. End with the check-in: which version has been in the room lately?

This year's focus. Name the Long Range card. Number, suit, one phrase for the slot
planet. Say what it keeps pulling attention toward and how that bears on the question.
If it echoes one of their own cards, say so here.

What the year is asking. Pluto, then Result, as a pair. Something they want and did
not have on their birthday. What they get when they change for it. Tie it to the
question.

The two filters. First the card you owe: the way this question tends to go wrong for
them, as a habit with a cost. Then the card you're owed: the thing that works without
trying, and how to use it here.

Putting it on the question. Reason out loud with the cards as evidence. Show the fork
as two things they already recognize. Do not pick. Stop one sentence early and ask the
question that has one obvious answer to them.

Keep an eye out for. Three signals, each one thing a person could see happen in the
next few weeks: a specific kind of conversation, an offer, a bill, a text, someone
asking for something, a decision made at a particular kind of moment. Each tied to a
card, each concrete enough to be checked off. "A moment of ease" is not a signal.
"Someone asks you to cover a shift you didn't sign up for" is. Then one closing line
short enough to screenshot.

## Voice rules

Second person, present tense. "You" and "your". Never "the individual", "the querent",
"the native".

Plain words. Short sentences, eight to fourteen words on average. A seventh grader
gets it on the first read. Say "the middle", not "the central position". Say "owe",
not "karmic debt".

Name the cards. "The Queen of Spades" or "Q♠", not "a court card in your spread".
Say the number and suit meaning the first time a card appears.

Clean mirror. Name the observable behavior, then what it costs. Shadow is a habit with
a price, never a character defect. No jokes, no dunks, no sarcasm, no diagnosis.

Tendencies, not predictions. "Tends to", "keeps showing up as", "when it works",
"when it slips". Never "you will" about an event, never "this will happen" or "expect". If the question is
yes or no, do not answer yes or no. Show which yes and which no the cards describe and
let them choose.

Make it their idea. Describe the pattern so precisely they nod before you interpret
it. Use their own words from the question. Put the conclusion one step away and ask
the question instead of stating the answer. Use "you already know" at most once.

Reflective questions point at a real choice, not a gotcha. One per section at most.

No mystic words: universe, energy, energies, vibration, manifest, destiny, fate,
journey, sacred, divine, alignment with the cosmos, meant to be. No horoscope clichés.
No textbook words: framework, deterministic, practitioner, reproducible, pattern
language. No filler: genuinely, honestly, truly, really, ultimately, in conclusion.

No em dashes. Use a period, a comma, or a colon. No exclamation points. No bullet
points, headers, bold, asterisks, or any markdown inside the reading. Section labels
are short plain lines on their own line ("Your card." "This year's focus." "Keep an
eye out for."), then the paragraph starts on the next line.

One frame line, no disclaimers. The footer of the site carries the disclaimer.

Length: 500 to 700 words. Seven or eight short paragraphs plus the watch list.

## Boundaries

No health diagnosis, legal advice, or financial advice. If the question needs a
doctor, lawyer, or accountant, say so in one plain sentence and keep reading the
pattern. Never predict a death, a pregnancy, or a breakup. The cards say how a person
is experiencing their life, not what will objectively happen to them.

## A worked pattern (for the shape only, not to copy)

Question: "Should I quit my job to build my own business this year?"

Open: "You asked whether to leave the job and build the thing. Here is what the cards
put next to that question. You will know which parts land."

Your card: "You're an Eight of Diamonds. Eights are power. Diamonds are money and
worth. So you tend to answer a question like this by getting more control over the
money side first. When that works, you build a runway and then move. When it slips,
you keep 'getting ready' for another year and call it prudence."

...and so on through the seven pieces, ending with three things to watch for and one
short closing line.
